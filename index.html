Observatory for the Conservation of Mediterranean Architecture

01 / About

What — What is OCMA?

OCMA is an observatory dedicated to the study, understanding and dissemination of Mediterranean built heritage, considered through its material, cultural and social values.

Why — Why does it exist?

OCMA is founded on the belief that knowledge is the prerequisite for every form of conservation. Understanding a heritage asset means understanding its material fabric, history and transformations, as well as the role it plays within society and the communities that inhabit, use and recognise it as part of their identity.

Who — Who is it for?

Scholars, professionals, institutions and communities. OCMA aims to connect specialist research with shared knowledge, considering heritage not as an isolated object, but as part of a wider system of relationships between places and people.

Where — What is its field of observation?

The Mediterranean, understood not as a rigid geographical boundary but as a space of relations and exchange. A system shaped over time by people, materials, techniques, knowledge and architectural models, whose connections extend beyond the countries that directly face the sea.

When — When does conservation take place?

Every day. Conservation is a continuous process of observation, monitoring, prevention, maintenance, use and care. Restoration operates within this process when a specific action on the material fabric of a heritage asset becomes necessary.

How — How does OCMA operate?

By observing, studying, processing and disseminating. The Observatory gathers data, sources and testimonies, interprets them through research, and returns them to the public through communication and in-depth analysis.

What for — What is the objective?

To build awareness of the value of Mediterranean heritage, so that it can be recognised not only as a material inheritance, but also as a cultural and social resource. Conservation means sustaining the relationship between heritage, places and the communities that continue to shape its meaning over time.
